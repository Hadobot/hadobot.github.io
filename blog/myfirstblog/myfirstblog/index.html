Kickstarting My Article Writing Journey

Sep 5, 2025 - 6 minute read

This is my very first blog post, and instead of diving into a technical topic straight away, I want to share why I’m even starting this journey of writing and publishing in the first place. Over the past few months, I’ve had conversations with my friend my uncle, some of my seniors, and my teacher. All these discussions circled back to one powerful idea: document what you do.

At first, I wasn’t entirely convinced. Why should I spend time writing when I could just keep learning, building, and moving on to the next exciting thing? But the more I thought about it — and the more I listened to their advice — the more it clicked. This post is my attempt to put those thoughts together, share my motivation, and set the stage for what’s to come here.

How I Usually Learn

I am currently in my third semester of B.Tech in Electronics and Computer Engineering. Like most of us, I learn best by doing. I watch videos, read a lot, and work on projects. When I get interested in something, I try to implement it quickly and make something useful out of it.

I used to jump from one project to the next without ever pausing to document what I had learned along the way. The code was written, the result was achieved — and then I would move on. Sure, I remembered the broad concepts, but when I looked back, the finer details were often gone.

This became especially clear when I tried explaining what I had done to others. Sometimes, I struggled to break down the idea as clearly as I thought I could. Other times, I completely forgot small but important steps that had made the project work in the first place. In short, I wasn’t creating a reliable reference for myself or anyone else.

That’s where writing comes in. Documenting my work not only makes it easier to revisit later but also forces me to organize my thoughts and explain them in a way that others and my future self can understand.

The Advice That Stuck With Me

One of the turning points was a long discussion I had with my uncle. He shared how, in his early career, he also moved fast from one idea to the next. But eventually, he realized that writing about his projects helped him learn more effectively and articulate his knowledge better. When he explained this, I could see exactly what he meant.

Around the same time, I had a conversation with my teacher, who guided me through a project during my internship. He emphasized the importance of documenting everything I did, suggesting that I could refer back to my notes later. That advice resonated with me. It’s one thing to complete tasks and move on; it’s another to create a record of my experiences that I can revisit.

By starting to document my activities from the very beginning, I found that this practice became invaluable. Whenever I encountered a challenge or something went wrong, I could easily look back at my previous notes. This not only helped me troubleshoot effectively but also reinforced my understanding of the processes involved.

Shortly after, I had another enlightening conversation with my friend from school. We discussed how we should approach writing articles and what topics we should cover. As we brainstormed, we realized that starting with an introductory article could be a great way to kick off our writing journey. This would allow us to share our motivations and set the stage for what was to come. The idea excited us, and we felt that it would not only help us clarify our thoughts but also engage others who might be on a similar path.

Why I’m Choosing to Blog Publicly

Of course, I could just write in a private notebook. But putting my writing out on a blog has a few big advantages:

  1. Accountability – When I publish something online, I feel responsible for making sure it’s clear, accurate, and helpful. That extra pressure pushes me to think deeper.
  2. Future Reference – A blog is like a personal knowledge base. I can come back months later to revisit my own work. Instead of searching through old folders or relying on memory, I’ll have a record waiting for me.
  3. Sharing the Journey – I know there are students and beginners out there who might be walking the same path. If my notes, mistakes, or insights can help even one of them, then that’s a win.
  4. Opportunities to Connect – Blogs are not just one-way communication. They open doors for conversations, feedback, and even collaborations with people who share similar interests.

I’ve also realized that the act of sharing itself can be motivating. Writing turns a solo activity into a conversation with a wider community.

My Areas of Interest

Since this is an introductory post, let me share a bit about the areas I’m most curious about right now. Even though my degree is in Electronics and Computer Engineering, I find myself particularly drawn toward topics on the software side — things like cybersecurity, machine learning, and artificial intelligence.

These fields are vast, and I know I’m just scratching the surface. But that’s exactly why I want to blog: to record my progress as I learn and experiment. Some posts might be about small projects. Others might be reflections on concepts I find fascinating or confusing. Over time, I hope this blog becomes a mix of both — projects and perspectives.

What I Hope to Gain

I’m not expecting instant results or a huge audience overnight. Instead, I see this blog as an investment:

  • A way to learn more deeply.
  • A tool to look back and trace my growth.
  • A platform to showcase my work when opportunities come my way.
  • And maybe, a space to spark discussions with people who are curious about the same things.

I’ve also accepted that I’ll make mistakes along the way. Some posts might feel too simple, others might get technical details wrong, and that’s okay. The point isn’t perfection — it’s progress.

Looking Ahead

This is just the beginning. I don’t yet know how often I’ll be posting or what the exact mix of topics will look like. What I do know is that I want to commit to the practice of writing. Over time, I believe this habit will not only sharpen my technical skills but also improve how I communicate ideas.

If you’ve made it this far, thank you for reading my very first post! This blog is going to be a mix of projects, experiments, and reflections as I move through my engineering journey. If any of that sounds interesting, I’d love for you to follow along. And if you’ve been through a similar experience, I’d be excited to hear your thoughts and advice.

Here’s to learning, sharing, and growing — one post at a time.
